## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Strongly authored for Brain Atlas. The luminous anatomical rendering, quiet scientific instrumentation, evidence disclosures, editorial lesson rail, and disciplined Neural Cyan signal language could not be transplanted unchanged into an unrelated product. The wide lesson composition is the clearest expression of the system: a readable editorial rail beside a larger sticky anatomy stage. The main missed opportunity is not identity but orchestration—Atlas Home presents the full instrument panel before establishing the user’s immediate exploration goal.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json index.html` returned **0 findings** (`[]`, exit 0). There were no detector false positives. The deterministic scan confirms that the markup avoids the mechanical anti-patterns it knows, but it does not evaluate the cognitive density of the runtime-generated Viewer rail or the lesson’s missing completion state.

**Visual overlays:** Mutable injection preflight succeeded, but `http://localhost:8400/detect.js` reported a load failure and created no overlay nodes. The page’s strict same-origin script CSP prevents a reliable cross-port overlay, so **no user-visible detector overlay is available**. Browser proof frames were captured instead for wide lesson, compact lesson, Atlas Home, and the Lessons drawer.

## Overall Impression

This already feels like a real scientific instrument and a real teaching environment, not a themed WebGL demo. Its strongest mode—guided lesson reading—is confident, legible, and emotionally distinctive. The biggest opportunity is to make Atlas Home reveal its sophistication in layers instead of presenting the complete control ontology at once.

## What’s Working

1. **The interface is inseparable from the subject.** The anatomy remains the primary artifact; structural borders, quiet typography, and scarce cyan signals frame evidence without turning the page into decorative science fiction.
2. **The read/operate composition is excellent.** At 1440×1000, the roughly 586px reader and 771px sticky stage establish a clear educational rhythm; at 390×844, the stage-first compact sequence remains contained and preserves 44px controls.
3. **Honesty and recovery are first-class design features.** Model & sources, scene limitations, readable fallbacks, image retry, local validation, and preserved state build trust instead of hiding technical limits.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Atlas Home exposes the full control ontology before the user has chosen a task

**Why it matters:** The default 380px Viewer rail presents two hemisphere choices, eight structure groups, two tract groups, pathways, scene layers, endpoint filtering, playback, three sliders, four views, six camera actions, and reset. The groups are orderly, but this still forces a first-timer to understand several control systems simultaneously. The anatomy remains beautiful, yet the rail communicates “configure everything” rather than “inspect one thing.”

**Fix:** Keep the complete canonical controls, but stage them. Lead with a compact task layer—authored presets, hemisphere, activity, and view—then disclose Layers, Fibre endpoints, and Camera as separate task-oriented sections. Preserve the existing Viewer summary as the master collapse.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] The lesson ends in content, not in an interface completion state

**Why it matters:** The conclusion includes a strong recap and final retrieval prompt, but the transport simply disables **Next**. The learner gets no confirmation, no visible return to the opening prediction, and no deliberate choice to review, restart, or carry the scene into Atlas exploration. This weakens the peak-end effect after an otherwise carefully authored journey.

**Fix:** Turn the final transport state into a compact completion panel: “Lesson complete,” revisit the opening prediction, review scenes, restart, or explore the final scene in Atlas. Do not add gamified confetti; keep the tone evidential and restrained.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P2] Compact lesson opening has too many equally available actions before the prose begins

**Why it matters:** At 390×844, users encounter **Back to atlas**, **Open lesson**, **Explore this scene**, **Model & sources**, **Inspect anatomy**, **Skip transition**, and the fixed Previous/Next transport around a 378px stage before meaningful prose. All targets are accessible and contained, but the hierarchy does not identify one dominant next action.

**Fix:** Make “continue reading / next scene” the dominant compact path. Demote source disclosure and import to quieter treatments, show Skip only while a transition is actually consequential, and consider moving Inspect anatomy behind one stage disclosure on the opening scene.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Rich expert capability lacks a fast expert retrieval path

**Why it matters:** Keyboard users can operate the interface, but experts still traverse a long control rail. There is no visible shortcut help, layer search, “show only this,” or fast command path. This is an efficiency gap, not an accessibility failure.

**Fix:** Start with a small, discoverable shortcut/help surface and layer filtering. Add only accelerators that map to existing canonical actions; do not create a second control system.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** Alex can skip transitions, use semantic controls, and manipulate every axis, but still has to scan or tab through a long rail. No shortcut reference, layer search, saved view, or fast “solo this structure” action is visible. The endpoint presets help only one part of the workflow.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** Jordan reaches Atlas Home and sees labels such as **Unparcellated (GapMap)**, **Superficial fibres**, **Cutaway**, and **Tissue** before a task explains why to change them. The Lessons drawer is clear, but Atlas Home does not recommend whether to inspect anatomy, choose a preset, open sources, or manipulate layers first.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):** Sam benefits from excellent labels, live regions, focus styling, 44px targets, and keyboard-operable controls. The red flag is traversal cost: the accessibility tree contains many group checkboxes, disclosures, filters, sliders, view actions, and camera actions in one open panel. More progressive disclosure would reduce screen-reader and switch-control effort.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** Casey gets large touch targets and thumb-reachable fixed transport, but the compact opening spends 61px on transport and 378px on the stage while several top/stage actions compete. Returning after interruption is well supported; deciding what to do in the first viewport is less clear.

## Minor Observations

- **Explore this scene** and **Model & sources** carry almost equal visual weight despite very different intent and frequency.
- **Open lesson** reads like a primary learning action even while a checked lesson is already active; “Open local lesson” would better match its actual role.
- The Atlas interaction hint at the bottom of the stage is useful but visually remote from the Viewer controls that alter the same model.
- The Lessons drawer is exceptionally clear and restrained; its hierarchy is a good model for restructuring Viewer controls.

## Questions to Consider

- Should Atlas Home optimize first for guided inspection, or should it expose every scientific control immediately?
- What should a learner feel and do in the ten seconds after completing the final retrieval prompt?
- Which single action deserves visual dominance in the compact lesson’s first viewport?
- Could the Lessons drawer’s two-level simplicity become the organizational model for Viewer controls?
